## Part I · Vision, Brand & Identity

### 1 · The Vision in One Breath

**ReeferDude is the front door. PotGrowHub is the shared platform engine. The Socket is the connected journey.** Help people move from an idea to a useful, verified next step across learning, planning, design, sourcing, and compliant commerce. Trust, usefulness, and evidence come before scale.

### 2 · Naming: Who Is Who

ReeferDude is the public flagship; PotGrowHub is the underlying platform; The Socket is the connected-platform concept; GanjaGuru is the intelligence engine; GanjaGroot is internal orchestration; Dream-It Studio is creation; Ghost Commerce is the zero-inventory commerce capability. Public-facing AI/persona naming remains subject to the founder's recorded decision. Do not treat unresolved names as final branding.

### 3 · Founders, Audience & The Three Doors

The project is owned by Raymond “Ray” Meadows and Jessica Mullins. The intended audience is adults of legal age in their jurisdiction. Entry journeys are organized around three jobs: learn/grow, create/design, or operate a business. Age and location requirements must be resolved and implemented before any restricted service launches.

### 4 · Gravity-Gone: The Arrival Experience

A calm, cosmic visual layer can make the platform memorable, but it is progressive enhancement. Core content and navigation remain available in a clear linear layout, work without WebGL, support keyboard and touch, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Spatial effects never outrank usability.

### 5 · The 13 Modules

The planned module map covers The Stash, Dream-It Studio, Strain Lab, AI Budtender, Grow Room, Blueprint Engine, Kush Courier, Puff Quest, Brain Library, Consultation, Community, Business Hub, and User Vault. Each module must show its real status—live, demo, planned, or gated—and must not imply unavailable checkout, delivery, or integrations.

### 6 · The Socket: Service Flow

A user states a goal; the system clarifies it; offers an evidence-aware plan; supports design, learning, or sourcing; and asks for human approval before consequential actions. Orders, payment, fulfillment, and regulated services remain disabled until their legal, provider, and operational gates are satisfied.

### 7 · Brand System: Colors, Fonts & Emoji

Use the established obsidian, emerald, gold, purple, cyan, and warm-neutral palette. Typography prioritizes readability, and emoji supplement rather than replace labels. Visual effects remain restrained, responsive, and accessible.

### 8 · Knowledge Rules: The AI Never Invents

Label information as verified, estimated, or generated; preserve conflicts; cite sources where available; do not fabricate prices, product availability, statistics, legal conclusions, environmental claims, or medical outcomes. Simulations must be identified as estimates, not promises. Health and legal questions require appropriate professional or official-source handoff.

### 9 · Ghost Commerce: Zero-Inventory Engine

The commerce concept sources products only after demand and human approval. Suppliers and sustainability claims require evidence and due diligence. No automated purchasing or payment execution is enabled by this documentation; future workflows need permission checks, audit logs, and named human approval.

### 10 · Compliance: Blocked, Not Disclaimed

Eligibility is a gate, not fine print. Product category, destination, customer eligibility, and sales channel must be evaluated before a transaction path is offered. Rules are jurisdiction-specific; qualified legal review is required before regulated commerce or age-restricted features launch.

### 11 · Business Model & Money Map

Potential revenue includes compliant commerce, creator services, memberships, consultations, education, and B2B software. These are hypotheses until validated. The first milestone is proving one useful, repeatable customer workflow with transparent costs and no unsupported revenue claims.

### 12 · SEO / AEO: The Money Loop

Publish answer-first pages that solve real questions, make evidence inspectable, use accurate metadata and structured data, and provide one useful next action. Page count is driven by verified content—not a target. Analytics must measure meaningful actions with appropriate consent.

### 13 · Tech Stack & Platform Status

The proposed direction is React/TypeScript/Vite, a modular Node/Express backend, PostgreSQL, and optional WebGL, local AI, and PWA capabilities. This is a plan, not proof of deployment. Keep provider integrations replaceable; never place secrets in browser code.

### 14 · Roadmap & Do-Not-Build List

Build the accessible foundation and prove a single useful workflow before expanding. Do not build a huge marketplace, automated payments, national delivery claims, medical advice, attention-harvesting loops, or large volumes of unsupported content before the prerequisites exist.

### 15 · Open Decisions: Ray's Calls

The source material records open decisions around age threshold, public AI name, domain hierarchy, hosting model, brand voice, and scale versus proof. Keep these visible and do not silently resolve disagreements. A founder-approved decision log should record each ruling and date.

### 16 · Glossary: The Brand Dictionary

Explain platform, commerce, AI, horticulture, and accessibility terms in plain language where they first appear. Keep a maintained glossary so user-facing language remains clear and consistent.

### 17 · FAQ

Answer common questions about whether cannabis is sold, what the demo AI can do, what is operational, how supplier claims are checked, how user data is handled, and why checkout or delivery may be unavailable. Answers must track actual product status.

### 18 · Link Directory

Keep official domains, repositories, source documents, and public product links in one curated directory. Check links and label private/local paths as internal references rather than public destinations.

### 19 · Honest Status: What Is Actually Live

Maintain a dated status table distinguishing production, preview, local-only, planned, and unavailable work. Include checkout, deployment, AI providers, analytics, catalog, and domain status only after verification.

### 20 · Credits, Ownership & Footer

Credit the founders and contributors accurately. Treat names, marks, artwork, and system designs as proprietary only where ownership is established; verify trademark and licensing claims before publication.

### 21 · The Mythology: The Modern Outlaw & The Green Grid

The brand story joins independent creativity, practical engineering, education, and care for the environment. Mythology is creative framing—not evidence of product safety, environmental impact, legal status, or business performance. Avoid stereotypes and glorification of unlawful conduct.

### 22 · The Persona Council: Who's on the AI Team

Planned specialist roles include general guide, product/knowledge helper, design assistant, grow-education helper, and business-planning assistant. They share common rules: source facts, state uncertainty, protect privacy, avoid medical/legal conclusions, and hand off consequential decisions to people.

### 23 · Dream-It Studio: Deep Dive

The studio roadmap covers configurable concepts, saved drafts, previews, material information, and export workflows. A visual mockup is not a manufacturing-ready design. Production readiness requires valid files, documented material/process constraints, supplier confirmation, and human approval.

### 24 · Strain Lab: Deep Dive

The strain library is an educational data project. Records need provenance, confidence labels, conflict handling, and import validation. Effects, lineage, cannabinoid levels, or cultivation figures must not be presented as verified without reliable sources. No treatment or dosage recommendations.

### 25 · Gamification System: Full Specification

Rewards may recognize learning, creation, and helpful contributions. Use a transparent, auditable ledger with user-controlled limits and clear rules. Do not reward consumption, use gambling mechanics, imply cash value, or create pressure loops without explicit legal and product review.

### 26 · Membership: Tier Deep Dive

Membership tiers and example prices in source notes are proposals until confirmed. Before offering a plan, define real benefits, billing terms, cancellation, refunds, taxes, eligibility, support, and payment-provider approval. Never imply a subscription is active when it is not.

### 27 · The Page Universe: Full Inventory

Treat the large route inventory as a planning backlog, not a launch checklist. Deduplicate pages into user journeys and build routes when they solve a documented need with maintainable content. Do not publish thin, duplicated, or speculative pages for volume.

### 28 · Root Files Cookbook

Root-level files have distinct jobs: `robots.txt` guides crawling; sitemap lists real public URLs; manifest identifies the installable app; service worker caches approved resources; `llms.txt` describes useful public content; `security.txt` provides a vulnerability-reporting contact; `humans.txt` credits contributors. Publish `ads.txt` only with verified publisher authorization. Never invent verification tokens, policies, or URLs.

### 29 · Accessibility & Inclusion Standard

Use semantic HTML, logical heading order, visible focus, keyboard-operable controls, accessible names, captions/transcripts, adequate contrast, zoom support, reduced motion, and a linear alternative to spatial navigation. Treat WCAG conformance as a testable requirement, not a marketing claim before audit.

### 30 · Security, Privacy & Trust

Minimize personal data, define retention and deletion, secure sessions, validate input, and keep credentials server-side. Do not store payment-card details or identity documents in application logs. Explain local storage, AI processing, analytics, and any third-party data sharing before enabling them.

### 31 · Analytics & The Owner Dashboard

Track only defined, useful events such as search, tool completion, signup, referrals, and confirmed transactions. Use consent and retention controls where required. Dashboard figures must come from measured data; never display fictional counters as live statistics.

### 32 · Community & Content Engine

Community features require moderation, reporting, privacy controls, and clear content rules. Publish authentic creator work only with permission. Do not fabricate testimonials, engagement, events, or community members.

### 33 · Competitive Landscape

Compare alternatives using current, cited evidence and a clear date/method. Focus on the customer problem and differentiated utility rather than unsupported “best,” “first,” or market-size claims.

### 34 · Risk Register & Kill Criteria

Track legal, safety, privacy, security, supplier, payment, operational, and reputation risks with owners and mitigations. Pause or remove a feature when evidence, authorization, security, or operating capacity is insufficient. Define measurable stop criteria before pilots.

## Part II · Engineering & Infrastructure

### 35 · Complete System Architecture

Begin with an accessible frontend and a small modular backend only when needed. Separate UI, domain logic, persistence, and provider adapters. External services must fail gracefully without preventing the public informational experience from loading.

### 36 · Master File & Folder Architecture

Use one authoritative app entry point, one design system, one route map, and one source of truth per data domain. Generate repetitive assets from validated sources instead of creating hundreds of empty files. The repository's file map is a planning aid, not a claim that every listed file exists.

### 37 · AI Agent Architecture & Orchestration

Agents are scoped services, not autonomous authorities. Define allowed tools, input/output schemas, user consent, data boundaries, timeouts, audit events, and human-approval gates. Never let an agent make a payment, publish a claim, or commit a supplier order without explicit authorized approval.

### 38 · API & Integration Directory

For each integration, document purpose, owner, data exchanged, scopes, secret location, cost, rate limits, test mode, health check, failure behavior, and cancellation path. No integration is “connected” until configured and tested in its real environment.

### 39 · Database & Data Architecture

Use PostgreSQL as the proposed authoritative application store, with tenant boundaries enforced server-side and database-side where appropriate. Define migrations, constraints, indexes, backups, deletion, and retention. Add vector or time-series stores only when actual requirements justify them. Do not store sensitive data without a reviewed need.

### 40 · Hosting, Deployment & Infrastructure

The target is a provider-independent production application, with local development as the workshop. Hosting, DNS, TLS, backups, secrets, release process, rollback, and environment configuration must be documented and tested. A build artifact is not a deployment.

### 41 · Offline-First & Synchronization Engine

Cache the app shell and explicitly approved public content. Save user work locally with clear device-storage disclosure. Queue only safe, retryable operations; resolve conflicts visibly. Payments, live supplier availability, account sync, and cloud AI require connectivity unless a separately reviewed local implementation exists.

### 42 · Testing, QA & Quality Assurance

Require type checks, unit tests, API/integration tests, accessibility checks, production builds, and key browser/device journeys. Test offline and error states. Report what passed and what was not tested; do not turn plans or test placeholders into completion claims.

### 43 · DevOps, Monitoring & Disaster Recovery

Use repeatable builds, dependency updates, least-privilege access, secret rotation, health checks, logs without unnecessary personal data, and tested backups. Document incident response, restore exercises, and rollback. Avoid promising uptime until it is measured and supported.

## Part III · Commerce & Revenue Operations

### 44 · Complete Commerce & Order Lifecycle

The planned flow is discovery, eligibility checks, transparent offer, customer approval, payment through an approved provider, supplier order, fulfillment updates, support, and refunds. Until all gates are live, provide request or waitlist pathways rather than simulated checkout.

### 45 · Supplier & Vendor Network

Supplier onboarding requires identity and capability checks, written terms, product evidence, fulfillment history, geographic coverage, and a process for keeping claims current. Maintain audit dates and block suppliers whose evidence expires or fails review.

### 46 · Product Catalog & Inventory Intelligence

Represent product facts, source, price timestamp, availability timestamp, restrictions, images, and claim evidence separately. A supplier feed or sample catalog is not proof of live stock. Do not list controlled or restricted goods without jurisdictional authorization.

### 47 · Payments, Payouts & Financial Operations

Payments remain disabled until a provider approves the actual business model and products in writing. Use provider-hosted collection where possible; never fake success, hold funds without legal structure, or expose payment secrets. Document reconciliation, refunds, chargebacks, taxes, and payout permissions before launch.

### 48 · Affiliate, Referral & Partnership Engine

Clearly disclose affiliate relationships and sponsored placements. Track referrals only with appropriate consent and attribution rules. Do not imply endorsement or guarantee commissions, and do not publish partner claims without verification.

### 49 · Revenue Attribution & Profitability

Separate measured revenue from estimates and targets. Account for refunds, fees, fulfillment, support, taxes, and acquisition costs. Internal transfers between related entities should not be double-counted in consolidated reporting.

### 50 · Customer Relationship Management

Collect only the contact and preference data required for a stated purpose. Provide consent, unsubscribe, access, correction, and deletion pathways as applicable. Keep support records access-controlled and define retention.

## Part IV · AI Intelligence & Automation

### 51 · AI Gateway, Models & Provider Management

Use a provider-neutral server-side adapter with explicit configuration, usage limits, timeouts, and safe fallback behavior. No provider is configured by default. Never expose API keys in frontend bundles or promise model capabilities that have not been tested.

### 52 · AI Memory, Context & Personalization

Memory is opt-in, inspectable, editable, and deletable. Explain what is saved, why it is used, and how long it remains. Prefer session context or local-only preferences until durable memory has a clear user benefit and privacy review.

### 53 · Autonomous Workflow & Automation Engine

Start with drafts and recommendations. Automations require named permissions, least privilege, idempotency, rate limits, audit records, and a safe stop. Human approval is mandatory for payment, purchase, refunds, publication, sensitive exports, and supplier commitments.

### 54 · Knowledge Base & RAG Infrastructure

Retrieve only from an approved, versioned corpus with source metadata, timestamps, and confidence. Show citations or source names where feasible. Handle missing or conflicting sources by stating uncertainty rather than fabricating a confident answer.

### 55 · Voice, Speech & Multimodal Intelligence

Voice and file features are optional, permission-based, and have text alternatives. Explain microphone/camera access and external processing before use. Do not infer sensitive health attributes or make medical diagnoses from images, audio, or user text.

## Part V · Growth, Ownership & Long-Term Value

### 56 · MVP Scope & Acceptance Criteria

The first release should deliver a clear first-step CTA, useful source-aware assistant demo, one practical planning tool, responsive accessible navigation, and honest status labels. Acceptance criteria include keyboard use, mobile layout, no fabricated live integrations, and successful production build.

### 57 · User Journeys & Experience Blueprints

Document journeys for learner, creator, grow-room planner, and merchant. For each, define entry point, task, data needed, consent, success measure, failure route, and human handoff. Keep the linear journey usable without spatial effects.

### 58 · Marketing, Acquisition & Retention Engine

Use useful education, opt-in communications, transparent partnerships, and measurable customer outcomes. Avoid paid attention loops, fabricated scarcity, fake reviews, undisclosed sponsorships, and unsupported traffic or ranking promises.

### 59 · Expansion, Localization & Internationalization

Add regions only after language, accessibility, legal, tax, shipping, privacy, and support requirements are reviewed. Store locale and jurisdiction explicitly where needed, minimize location data, and never assume one region's rules apply globally.

### 60 · Intellectual Property & Platform Defensibility

Maintain an asset and license register for code, fonts, images, datasets, models, names, and creator submissions. Obtain permission for third-party content and user work. Defensibility should come from useful workflows, trustworthy data operations, and earned partnerships—not unverified IP claims.

### 61 · Standard Operating Procedures & Administration

Document account provisioning, content review, supplier review, incident escalation, moderation, data requests, backups, and release approval. Admin actions use least privilege and audit logging; sensitive operations require a second authorized person where appropriate.

### 62 · Scalability, Capacity & Performance Engineering

Measure real load times, error rates, accessibility, and device performance before optimizing. Lazy-load optional spatial effects, provide a lightweight fallback, and set budgets from measured baselines. Do not claim a frame rate, response time, or scale capacity without testing.

### 63 · Valuation, Strategic Partnerships & Exit Strategy

Business valuations and revenue mixes in planning notes are scenarios, not appraisals or forecasts. Use transparent assumptions and independently verifiable financials. Evaluate partnerships by customer benefit, compliance, control of user data, and sustainable economics.

### 64 · Master Documentation & Change Management

Keep this README as the project map, with decisions, status, owners, and dates updated as the build changes. Link detailed implementation guides rather than duplicating conflicting specs. Record material changes, source provenance, test evidence, and unresolved questions; retire stale claims instead of silently carrying them forward.
